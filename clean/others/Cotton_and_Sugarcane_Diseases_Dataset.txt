CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Fusarium Wilt
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. vasinfectum
SYMPTOMS: Affects the crop at all stages. Earliest symptoms appear on seedlings
as yellowing then browning of cotyledons; brown ring at base of petiole,
followed by wilting and drying of seedlings. In young/grown plants, first
symptom is yellowing of leaf edges and veins area, discoloration starting
from margin and spreading to midrib. Leaves lose turgidity, turn brown, droop
and drop off. Symptoms start from older leaves at the base and progress
upward to branches and whole plant. Defoliation/wilting may be complete
(only stem left standing) or partial. Taproot usually stunted. Browning/
blackening of vascular tissue with black streaks extending to branches and
lateral roots. In severe cases discoloration extends to stem, leaves and
bolls. Transverse section shows discoloured ring in woody stem tissue.
Late-season infected plants are stunted with fewer, small bolls that open
prematurely.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Soil temperature 20-30°C; hot and dry periods followed
by rains; heavy black soils with alkaline reaction; increased nitrogen and
phosphatic fertilizer doses; wounds caused by nematode (Meloidogyne
incognita) and grubs of Ash weevil (Myllocerus pustulatus).
DISEASE CYCLE: Fungus survives in soil as saprophyte for many years;
chlamydospores act as resting spores. Pathogen is both externally and
internally seed-borne. Primary infection mainly from dormant hyphae and
chlamydospores in soil. Secondary spread through conidia and chlamydospores
disseminated by wind and irrigation water.
MANAGEMENT:
- Treat acid delinted seeds with Carboxin or Carbendazim at 2 g/kg.
- Remove and burn infected plant debris after deep summer ploughing (June-July).
- Apply increased doses of potash with balanced nitrogen and phosphorus.
- Apply heavy doses of farm yard manure/organic manures; mixed cropping with
  non-host plants.
- Grow disease-resistant varieties.
- Spot drench with Carbendazim 1 g/litre.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Verticillium Wilt
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Verticillium albo-atrum
SYMPTOMS: Seen when crop is in squares and bolls; early-infected plants
severely stunted. First symptom is bronzing of veins, followed by
interveinal chlorosis and yellowing of leaves. Leaves dry giving a scorched
appearance; drying of leaf margins and interveinal areas gives "Tiger
stripe"/"Tiger claw" appearance. Affected leaves fall, leaving branches
barren. Infected stem/roots show pinkish discoloration of woody tissue,
tapering into longitudinal streaks. Infected leaves show brown spots at
petiole ends. Affected plants bear few smaller bolls with immature lint.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not separately specified (occurs during squares/boll
stage of crop growth).
DISEASE CYCLE: Fungus also infects brinjal, chilli, tobacco, etc. Survives
in infected plant debris and soil as micro sclerotia up to 14 years. Seeds
carry micro sclerotia and conidia in the fuzz. Primary spread through micro
sclerotia/conidia in soil. Secondary spread through root-to-root contact and
dissemination of infected plant parts via irrigation water and implements.
MANAGEMENT:
- Treat delinted seeds with Carboxin or Carbendazim at 2 g/kg.
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris after deep summer ploughing
  (June-July).
- Apply heavy doses of farm yard manure or compost at 100 t/ha.
- Crop rotation with paddy or chrysanthemum for 2-3 years.
- Spot drench with 0.05 g/l benomyl or carbendazim 500 mg/l.
- Grow disease-resistant varieties.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Root Rot
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Rhizoctonia solani
SYMPTOMS: Causes three symptom types - seedling disease, sore-shin, and root
rot. Germinating/1-2 week old seedlings attacked at hypocotyl causing black
lesions, stem girdling and death (large gaps in field). Sore-shin stage
(4-6 week old plants): dark reddish-brown cankers on stems near soil
surface, turning black; plant breaks at collar region, leaves dry and
plant dies. Typical root rot appears at maturity: sudden and complete
wilting of plants in patches, leaves droop and die within a day or two.
Roots show rotting of entire root system except tap root/few laterals.
Bark shreds and extends above ground; woody portions may become black and
brittle. Numerous dark brown sclerotia seen on wood/shredded bark.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Dry weather following heavy rains; high soil
temperature (35-39°C); cultivation of favourable hosts like vegetables,
oil seeds and legumes preceding cotton; wounds from ash weevil grubs and
nematodes.
DISEASE CYCLE: Mainly soil-borne; pathogen survives in soil as sclerotia for
several years. Spread through sclerotia disseminated by irrigation water,
implements, and other cultural operations.
MANAGEMENT:
- Treat seeds with Trichoderma viride @ 4 g/kg of seed.
- Spot drench with 0.1% Carbendazim.
- Apply farm yard manure at 10 t/ha or neem cake at 150 kg/ha.
- Adjust sowing time - early sowing (first week of April) or late sowing
  (last week of June) to escape high soil temperature.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Anthracnose
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Colletotrichum gossypii (anamorph); Glomerella gossypii
SYMPTOMS: Infects seedlings, producing small reddish circular spots on
cotyledons and primary leaves. Lesions develop on collar region; stem may
be girdled causing seedling to wilt and die. In mature plants, fungus
attacks stem causing splitting and shredding of bark. Most common symptom
is boll spotting: small water-soaked, circular, reddish-brown depressed
spots on bolls. Lint stained yellow/brown, becomes solid brittle fibre
mass. Infected bolls cease growth, burst, and dry up prematurely.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Prolonged rainfall at boll formation time; close
planting.
DISEASE CYCLE: Survives as dormant mycelium in seed or as conidia on seed
surface for about a year. Perpetuates on rotten bolls and plant debris in
soil. Secondary spread by air-borne conidia. Survives on weed hosts
Aristolochia bractiata and Hibiscus diversifolius.
MANAGEMENT:
- Treat delinted seeds with Carbendazim, Carboxin, Thiram or Captan at 2 g/kg.
- Remove and burn infected plant debris and bolls.
- Rogue out weed hosts.
- Spray at boll formation with Mancozeb 2 kg/ha, or Copper oxychloride
  2.5 kg/ha, or Carbendazim 500 g/ha.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Boll Rot
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal complex
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Fusarium moniliforme, Colletotrichum gossypii, Aspergillus
flavus, A. niger, Rhizopus nigricans, Nematospora nagpuri, Botryodiplodia sp.
(also listed elsewhere with Ascochyta gossypii, Diplodia gossypina,
Phytophthora spp., Rhizoctonia solani)
SYMPTOMS: Begins as small brown/black dots that enlarge to cover entire
bolls. Infection spreads to inner tissues; seeds and lint rot. Bolls never
burst open and fall off prematurely. Sometimes rotting is external (pericarp
rots, internal tissues remain free). Large numbers of fungal fruiting
bodies observed on affected bolls, depending on the fungi involved.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Heavy rainfall during square and boll formation;
wounds caused by insects, especially red cotton bug (Dysdercus cingulata);
close spacing and excessive nitrogen application.
DISEASE CYCLE: Fungi survive in infected bolls in soil. Insects mainly help
spread the disease by creating entry wounds. Secondary spread also via
air-borne conidia.
MANAGEMENT:
- Adopt optimum spacing.
- Apply recommended fertilizer doses.
- Spray Copper oxychloride 2.5 kg/ha with a bollworm insecticide from the
  45th day at 15-day intervals (2-3 sprays).

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Leaf Blight
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Alternaria macrospora
SYMPTOMS: Occurs at all stages, more severe at 45-60 days old. Small,
plate-to-brown, irregular/round spots (0.5-6 mm diameter) with a central
lesion surrounded by concentric rings. Spots coalesce into blighted areas;
affected leaves become brittle and fall off. Stem lesions sometimes seen;
in severe cases spots appear on bracts and bolls.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: High humidity; intermittent rains; moderate
temperature of 25-28°C.
DISEASE CYCLE: Survives in dead leaves as dormant mycelium. Primary spread
through irrigation water; secondary spread mainly by airborne conidia.
MANAGEMENT:
- Remove and destroy infected plant residues.
- Spray Mancozeb 2 kg/ha or Copper oxychloride 2 kg/ha at disease onset.
- Give 4-5 sprays at 15-day intervals.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Bacterial Blight
DISEASE GROUP: Bacterial
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Xanthomonas campestris pv. malvacearum
SYMPTOMS: Attacks all stages from seed to harvest, with five common phases:
1) Seedling blight - small water-soaked, circular/irregular lesions on
   cotyledons; infection spreads via petiole to stem causing withering and
   death of seedlings.
2) Angular leaf spot - small dark green water-soaked areas on lower leaf
   surface, enlarging into angular spots restricted by veins; turn reddish
   brown with age; spreads to veins/veinlets.
3) Vein blight / vein necrosis / black vein - blackening of veins/veinlets
   ("blighting" appearance); bacterial oozes form crusts/scales on lower
   leaf surface; leaves crinkle, twist inward, wither; spreads to petiole
   causing defoliation.
4) Black arm - dark brown to black lesions on stem/fruiting branches,
   girdling stem and branches; premature leaf drop, stem cracking, gummosis,
   stem breakage, hanging as dry black twig ("black arm" symptom).
5) Square rot / Boll rot - water-soaked lesions on bolls turning dark
   black, sunken, irregular; spreads to entire boll causing shedding;
   premature bursting of mature bolls; lint stained yellow by bacterial
   ooze, losing market value; seeds infected, reduced size/viability.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Optimum soil temperature 28°C; high atmospheric
temperature 30-40°C; relative humidity 85%; early sowing; delayed thinning;
poor tillage; late irrigation; potassium deficiency in soil; rain followed
by bright sunshine during October-November.
DISEASE CYCLE: Survives on infected, dried plant debris in soil for
several years. Seed-borne, persisting as slimy mass on seed coat fuzz.
Also attacks Thumbergia thespesioides, Eriodendron anfructuosum, and
Jatropha curcus. Primary infection mainly from seed-borne bacterium.
Secondary spread via wind, wind-blown rain splash, irrigation water,
insects, and implements.
MANAGEMENT:
- Delint cotton seeds with concentrated sulphuric acid at 100 ml/kg; treat
  delinted seeds with carboxin/oxycarboxin at 2 g/kg, or soak in 1000 ppm
  Streptomycin sulphate overnight.
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris.
- Rogue out volunteer cotton plants and weed hosts.
- Follow crop rotation with non-host crops.
- Early thinning and early earthing up with potash.
- Grow resistant varieties.
- Spray Streptomycin sulphate + Tetracycline mixture 100 g with Copper
  oxychloride at 1.25 kg/ha.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Leaf Curl Disease
DISEASE GROUP: Viral
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV)
SYMPTOMS: Downward and upward curling of leaves, thickening of veins, and
enation (outgrowth) on the underside of leaves. In severe infection all
leaves are curled and growth is retarded; boll-bearing capacity is reduced.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not separately specified; linked to whitefly vector
population buildup.
DISEASE CYCLE: Primary source is the viruliferous whitefly vector Bemisia
tabaci. Alternate and cultivated hosts serve as virus reservoirs throughout
the year.
MANAGEMENT:
- Time planting date to avoid peak vector population.
- Eliminate volunteer perennial cotton and alternate hosts including
  malvaceous weeds like wild okra.
- Foliar application of neem leaf extract and 1% neem oil (achieved ~80%
  reduction in virus transmission in trials).
- Vector management using granular systemic insecticides.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Red Rot
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Colletotrichum falcatum (anamorph); Glomerella
tucumanensis (perfect stage)
SYMPTOMS: Recognized when canes near maturity. First symptom is
discolouration of young leaves - typically 3rd/4th leaf from top turns
paler and droops slightly; tip dries and withers along the margin, central
area stays green. Leaves show dark red lesions on the mid-rib that
elongate and turn blood-red with dark margins; mature lesions have a
straw-coloured centre with black dot-like acervuli. Split canes give off
an alcoholic smell (fermentation); internal tissue is red, interrupted by
whitish patches in internodal tissue. Reddening most intense in vascular
bundles, extending to pith. Pith eventually dries, rind shrinks, canes
become lighter and break easily.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Monoculturing of sugarcane; successive ratoon
cropping; water-logged conditions and insect-caused injuries.
DISEASE CYCLE / PERPETUATION: Perpetuated year to year via infected planting
setts and fungus surviving on diseased canes/ratoon crop. Fungus may
persist in soil via diseased clumps/dry leaves left after harvest. Primary
infection mainly from infected setts; secondary infection via spores on
affected canes and leaf midribs. Secondary spread via irrigation water,
tools/implements, wind-borne inocula. Survives on collateral hosts Sorghum
vulgare, S. halepense, Saccharum spontaneum. Conidia invade leaves through
wounds including midrib splitting. Stem infection via borer tunnels, root
primordial, and seed cuttings; soil-borne fungus may enter healthy setts
through cut ends.
MANAGEMENT:
- Cultivate resistant varieties (e.g., Iswardi 2/54, Iswardi-16,
  Iswardi-17).
- Plant only healthy setts.
- Hot water treatment of setts at 52°C for 30 minutes.
- Dip setts momentarily in Aratan-6 solution (1/2 lb per 23 gallons water)
  before planting.
- Dip cut ends in coal tar before planting.
- Strictly avoid ratooning infected fields.
- Destroy plant debris and diseased material by burning after harvest.
- Control borers and improve field drainage.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Stem Canker / Wilt
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Cephalosporium sacchari (= Fusarium sacchari)
SYMPTOMS: First symptoms appear only after plant has grown 4-5 months.
Leaves of affected clumps gradually turn yellow and dry, causing wilting of
the cane. Longitudinal splitting reveals pith discoloured purple or dark
red near the node. A characteristic disagreeable odour is associated with
diseased canes. Cottony white mycelium may be seen in the pith region.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: High day temperature (30-35°C); low humidity
(50-60%); low soil moisture and alkaline soils; excess nitrogenous
fertilizer doses.
DISEASE CYCLE / DISSEMINATION: Fungus remains in diseased canes/plant
debris left in fields after harvest; ratoon crop helps fungus survive.
Transmitted via infected seed setts, air, rain water, and irrigation water.
Penetrates cane base and below-soil region through wounds. Can survive in
soil as saprophyte for 2-3 years; favoured by near-neutral/alkaline soils.
MANAGEMENT:
- Plant disease-free setts and use resistant varieties.
- Avoid alkaline soils for cultivation.
- Destroy cane plant debris (controls disease in alkali soils).
- Dip setts in Bavistin fungicide solution (0.1%) for 30 minutes before
  planting.
- Discourage ratooning of diseased crop.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: White Leaf Disease
DISEASE GROUP: MLO (Mycoplasma-like organism / Phytoplasma)
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Phytoplasma (MLO)
SYMPTOMS: Initially a single white/cream line parallel to the midrib.
Later, several straight white-to-light-green-to-yellow stripes develop
parallel to the midrib along the entire leaf length (rarely onto upper leaf
sheath). Mottled pattern of normal/light-green dot, spot, streak or patch
islands on a white background, varying in size/shape. Plant vigour
decreases as disease develops; stunted stalks, absence of side shoots on
upper stalk, and abnormal tillering.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not specified in source.
DISEASE CYCLE: Not specified in source (phytoplasma diseases are typically
spread by leafhopper vectors and infected planting material).
MANAGEMENT: Not specified in source document.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Whip Smut
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Ustilago scitaminea
SYMPTOMS: Affected canes produce long, black, whip-like, coiled/curved
shoots covered with a thin silvery membrane containing fungal spore masses.
Smutted shoots arise from the growing point of the cane. At maturity, the
whip ruptures, releasing millions of tiny black smut spores (teliospores)
dispersed by wind, contaminating soil and standing crop. Infected plants
may remain stunted with many tillers bearing small, narrow leaves.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Monoculturing of sugarcane; continuous ratooning and
dry weather during the tillering stage.
DISEASE CYCLE: Teliospores may survive in soil up to 10 years; spores also
present in infected plant material in soil, and smut spores/dormant
mycelium present in/on infected setts. Primary spread via diseased
seed-pieces (setts); soil-borne spores also spread via rain/irrigation
water. Secondary spread mainly through smut spores from whips, aided by air
currents. Survives on collateral hosts Saccharum spontaneum, S. robustum,
Sorghum vulgare, Imperata arundinacea, Cyperus dilatatus.
MANAGEMENT:
- Do not use setts from smutted canes for planting.
- Rogue out and burn smutted plants before spore release.
- Encourage use of resistant varieties.
- Avoid ratooning infected fields.
- 95% moist hot air at 54°C for 4 hours, or hot water treatment of setts at
  50°C for 3 hours, to eliminate internal infection.
- Disinfect seed-setts in 0.1% mercuric chloride or formaline solution for
  5 minutes, followed by 2 hours covered under a moist cloth (or other
  effective market chemicals).
- Crop rotation.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Ratoon Stunting Disease (RSD)
DISEASE GROUP: Bacterial (RLO - Rickettsia-like organism)
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Clavibacter xyli
SYMPTOMS: No specific external symptoms typical of most plant diseases.
External expression includes stunted growth, reduced tillering, thin
stalks with shortened internodes, and yellowish foliage. Longitudinal
slicing of a diseased stalk reveals discoloration at the node of mature
canes in the shape of dots or commas, and general pink colour in the nodes
of very young canes. Colour range includes yellow, orange, pink, red, and
reddish brown.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not separately specified; spread favoured by use of
contaminated cutting tools and infected planting material.
DISEASE CYCLE / DISSEMINATION: Spreads mainly through planting material;
setts should be selected from healthy plants. Also spread via knives,
sickles, or agricultural equipment used in the cane field.
MANAGEMENT:
- Adequate irrigation, particularly at early growth stages (plant crops)
  and in less susceptible varieties.
- Provide healthy planting material.
- Use resistant varieties.
- Hot water treatment of setts at 50°C for 3 hours.
- Sterilize cane knives with 20% "lysol" solution.
- Establish a clean seed nursery as a source of planting material.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Sett Rot / Pineapple Disease
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Ceratocystis paradoxa
SYMPTOMS: Primarily affects setts, usually 2-3 weeks after planting.
Diseased setts may rot before germination, or shoots may die after reaching
6-12 inches height. Affected tissues first turn reddish, then brownish
black, with sett contents rotting. Severely affected setts show internodal
cavities covered with mycelium and abundant spores. A characteristic
pineapple smell is associated with the rotting (hence the name). Surviving
infected shoots are very much stunted and chlorotic.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Poorly drained fields; heavy clay soils; temperature
of 25-30°C; prolonged rainfall after planting.
DISEASE CYCLE: Not separately detailed beyond symptom description; spreads
via infected setts and soil under favourable moist conditions.
MANAGEMENT:
- Obtain healthy setts from a disease-free field.
- Soak setts in 0.05% Carbendazim for 15 minutes before planting to protect
  cut ends.
- Pretreat setts with hot water to stimulate bud germination and hasten
  growth, helping plants outcompete the pathogen.
- Combine field sanitation with chemical pre-treatment of setts.
- Provide adequate drainage during rainy seasons.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Leaf Scald
DISEASE GROUP: Bacterial
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Xanthomonas albilineans
SYMPTOMS: Most typical symptom is a white pencil-line streak (1-2 mm wide)
on the leaf, extending from the midrib to the leaf margin, running parallel
to veins. As disease progresses, necrosis develops from the leaf tip or
margin. On mature stalks, side shoots appear from each node at the stalk
base and progress upward, usually showing scalding and/or white pencil
lines. Internally, affected stalks may show bright-to-dark red streaks from
vascular bundle necrosis. Acute phase: sudden wilting and death of mature
stalks, often without prior symptom expression.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not separately specified in source.
DISEASE CYCLE: Not separately detailed; spread by infected setts and
mechanical transmission via cutting tools.
MANAGEMENT:
- Prevention and replacement of susceptible varieties with resistant ones
  (best control).
- Long hot-water treatment of setts to kill the pathogen.
- Sterilize all cane cutting knives, including mechanical harvester blades,
  when coming from suspect fields, to prevent mechanical spread.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Mosaic
DISEASE GROUP: Viral
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Sugarcane Mosaic Virus (SCMV)
SYMPTOMS: Appears more prominently on the basal portion of younger foliage
as chlorotic/yellowish stripes alternating with normal green leaf portions.
As infection becomes severe, yellow stripes appear on the leaf sheath and
stalks. Elongated necrotic lesions develop on stalks, causing stem
splitting. Necrotic lesions also develop on internodes; entire plant
becomes stunted and chlorotic.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not separately specified.
DISSEMINATION: Transmitted through mosaic-infected setts and by an aphid
vector. Alternate hosts: maize and sorghum.
MANAGEMENT:
- Dig out and destroy infected plants by burning.
- Rogue out diseased clumps periodically.
- Plant virus-free setts; avoid ratooning diseased crop.
- Aerated Steam Therapy (AST) at 56°C for 3 hours for setts before
  planting.
- Grow resistant varieties.
- Timely control of the insect (aphid) vector.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Red-Stripe & Top Rot
DISEASE GROUP: Bacterial
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Pseudomonas rubrilineans
SYMPTOMS: Red stripe: chlorotic lesions carrying dark red stripes on
leaves, either distributed over the blade or concentrated in the middle;
several may coalesce to cover large leaf areas; initially water-soaked
green stripes, turning dark red on both leaf surfaces. In severe infection,
rotting commences at the tip and spreads downward, causing shoot/top rot.
Splitting affected canes downward reveals vascular bundles exuding foul-
smelling yellowish gum, with dark red tissue discoloration.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Continuous ratooning; prolonged rainy weather with
low temperature (25°C).
DISEASE CYCLE / DISSEMINATION: Spreads in the field via wind, rain, and
cutting. Bacterial cells falling on host plants enter through natural
openings or wounds and establish in various tissues, including the xylem.
MANAGEMENT:
- Difficult disease to control.
- Remove and burn affected plants as soon as noticed (systematic
  destruction reduces disease incidence).
- Grow resistant varieties (best method of control).
- Use disease-free certified setts.

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Root-Knot Disease
DISEASE GROUP: Nematode
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Meloidogyne javanica
SYMPTOMS: Diseased plants are chlorotic and stunted; yellow stripes show on
young leaves while older leaves appear healthy. Roots, when dug out, are
found knotted; nematodes are found inside the knots, feeding on the plant
juice; in some cases plants die.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not separately specified.
DISEASE DISSEMINATION: Nematode eggs persist in soil; female adults
associate with root-knot formation; upon planting a susceptible host, larval
forms invade the root and form galls, feeding through the vascular bundles
to complete their life cycle.
MANAGEMENT:
- Fumigate soil with a nematicide such as Carbofuran/Furadan 3G or 5G.
- Open up the soil soon after crop harvest.
- Soil is safe for planting 2-3 weeks after fumigation.
- Crop rotation with marigold (reduces nematode population in some
  countries).

CROP: Sugarcane
DISEASE NAME: Pokkah Boeng
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Fusarium moniliforme (anamorph); Gibberella fujikuroi
(perfect stage)
SYMPTOMS: Initial symptoms are chlorotic areas at the base of young leaves,
followed by distortion (wrinkling/twisting) and shortening of affected
leaves; in severe cases the stalk dies. Base of affected leaves often
narrower than normal. As leaves mature, irregular reddish stripes/specks
develop within chlorotic parts, sometimes forming lens-shaped holes with
no definite arrangement. Reddish tissue may form ladder-like lesions with
dark edges. Leaf sheaths may become chlorotic with irregular reddish
necrotic areas. Infection may continue down the spindle into the stalk,
producing dark reddish streaks through several internodes; internode
lesions may show cross depressions (ladder-like appearance) that break
through the rind surface. The causal fungi can also cause seedling blight,
scorch, stalk rot, root rot, wilt, and stunting in various crops.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: A wet season followed by dry climatic conditions;
sugarcane 3-7 months old and growing vigorously is most susceptible.
DISEASE CYCLE / SPREAD: Mainly by airborne spores; dissemination via seed
pieces occurs but is of little importance. Infection usually occurs through
the spindle along the margin of a partially unfolded leaf; spores germinate
and grow into inner spindle tissue, reaching the immature stem portion via
vascular bundles; may pass through leaf sheath vascular bundles without
entering surrounding tissue (ladder-like lesions in the sheath).
MANAGEMENT:
- Seldom serious enough to warrant control.
