
CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Fusarium Wilt
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. vasinfectum
SYMPTOMS: Affects the crop at all stages. Earliest symptoms appear on seedlings
as yellowing then browning of cotyledons; brown ring at base of petiole,
followed by wilting and drying of seedlings. In young/grown plants, first
symptom is yellowing of leaf edges and veins area, discoloration starting
from margin and spreading to midrib. Leaves lose turgidity, turn brown, droop
and drop off. Symptoms start from older leaves at the base and progress
upward to branches and whole plant. Defoliation/wilting may be complete
(only stem left standing) or partial. Taproot usually stunted. Browning/
blackening of vascular tissue with black streaks extending to branches and
lateral roots. In severe cases discoloration extends to stem, leaves and
bolls. Transverse section shows discoloured ring in woody stem tissue.
Late-season infected plants are stunted with fewer, small bolls that open
prematurely.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Soil temperature 20-30°C; hot and dry periods followed
by rains; heavy black soils with alkaline reaction; increased nitrogen and
phosphatic fertilizer doses; wounds caused by nematode (Meloidogyne
incognita) and grubs of Ash weevil (Myllocerus pustulatus).
DISEASE CYCLE: Fungus survives in soil as saprophyte for many years;
chlamydospores act as resting spores. Pathogen is both externally and
internally seed-borne. Primary infection mainly from dormant hyphae and
chlamydospores in soil. Secondary spread through conidia and chlamydospores
disseminated by wind and irrigation water.
MANAGEMENT:
- Treat acid delinted seeds with Carboxin or Carbendazim at 2 g/kg.
- Remove and burn infected plant debris after deep summer ploughing (June-July).
- Apply increased doses of potash with balanced nitrogen and phosphorus.
- Apply heavy doses of farm yard manure/organic manures; mixed cropping with
  non-host plants.
- Grow disease-resistant varieties.
- Spot drench with Carbendazim 1 g/litre.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Verticillium Wilt
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Verticillium albo-atrum
SYMPTOMS: Seen when crop is in squares and bolls; early-infected plants
severely stunted. First symptom is bronzing of veins, followed by
interveinal chlorosis and yellowing of leaves. Leaves dry giving a scorched
appearance; drying of leaf margins and interveinal areas gives "Tiger
stripe"/"Tiger claw" appearance. Affected leaves fall, leaving branches
barren. Infected stem/roots show pinkish discoloration of woody tissue,
tapering into longitudinal streaks. Infected leaves show brown spots at
petiole ends. Affected plants bear few smaller bolls with immature lint.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not separately specified (occurs during squares/boll
stage of crop growth).
DISEASE CYCLE: Fungus also infects brinjal, chilli, tobacco, etc. Survives
in infected plant debris and soil as micro sclerotia up to 14 years. Seeds
carry micro sclerotia and conidia in the fuzz. Primary spread through micro
sclerotia/conidia in soil. Secondary spread through root-to-root contact and
dissemination of infected plant parts via irrigation water and implements.
MANAGEMENT:
- Treat delinted seeds with Carboxin or Carbendazim at 2 g/kg.
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris after deep summer ploughing
  (June-July).
- Apply heavy doses of farm yard manure or compost at 100 t/ha.
- Crop rotation with paddy or chrysanthemum for 2-3 years.
- Spot drench with 0.05 g/l benomyl or carbendazim 500 mg/l.
- Grow disease-resistant varieties.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Root Rot
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Rhizoctonia solani
SYMPTOMS: Causes three symptom types - seedling disease, sore-shin, and root
rot. Germinating/1-2 week old seedlings attacked at hypocotyl causing black
lesions, stem girdling and death (large gaps in field). Sore-shin stage
(4-6 week old plants): dark reddish-brown cankers on stems near soil
surface, turning black; plant breaks at collar region, leaves dry and
plant dies. Typical root rot appears at maturity: sudden and complete
wilting of plants in patches, leaves droop and die within a day or two.
Roots show rotting of entire root system except tap root/few laterals.
Bark shreds and extends above ground; woody portions may become black and
brittle. Numerous dark brown sclerotia seen on wood/shredded bark.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Dry weather following heavy rains; high soil
temperature (35-39°C); cultivation of favourable hosts like vegetables,
oil seeds and legumes preceding cotton; wounds from ash weevil grubs and
nematodes.
DISEASE CYCLE: Mainly soil-borne; pathogen survives in soil as sclerotia for
several years. Spread through sclerotia disseminated by irrigation water,
implements, and other cultural operations.
MANAGEMENT:
- Treat seeds with Trichoderma viride @ 4 g/kg of seed.
- Spot drench with 0.1% Carbendazim.
- Apply farm yard manure at 10 t/ha or neem cake at 150 kg/ha.
- Adjust sowing time - early sowing (first week of April) or late sowing
  (last week of June) to escape high soil temperature.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Anthracnose
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Colletotrichum gossypii (anamorph); Glomerella gossypii
SYMPTOMS: Infects seedlings, producing small reddish circular spots on
cotyledons and primary leaves. Lesions develop on collar region; stem may
be girdled causing seedling to wilt and die. In mature plants, fungus
attacks stem causing splitting and shredding of bark. Most common symptom
is boll spotting: small water-soaked, circular, reddish-brown depressed
spots on bolls. Lint stained yellow/brown, becomes solid brittle fibre
mass. Infected bolls cease growth, burst, and dry up prematurely.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Prolonged rainfall at boll formation time; close
planting.
DISEASE CYCLE: Survives as dormant mycelium in seed or as conidia on seed
surface for about a year. Perpetuates on rotten bolls and plant debris in
soil. Secondary spread by air-borne conidia. Survives on weed hosts
Aristolochia bractiata and Hibiscus diversifolius.
MANAGEMENT:
- Treat delinted seeds with Carbendazim, Carboxin, Thiram or Captan at 2 g/kg.
- Remove and burn infected plant debris and bolls.
- Rogue out weed hosts.
- Spray at boll formation with Mancozeb 2 kg/ha, or Copper oxychloride
  2.5 kg/ha, or Carbendazim 500 g/ha.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Boll Rot
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal complex
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Fusarium moniliforme, Colletotrichum gossypii, Aspergillus
flavus, A. niger, Rhizopus nigricans, Nematospora nagpuri, Botryodiplodia sp.
(also listed elsewhere with Ascochyta gossypii, Diplodia gossypina,
Phytophthora spp., Rhizoctonia solani)
SYMPTOMS: Begins as small brown/black dots that enlarge to cover entire
bolls. Infection spreads to inner tissues; seeds and lint rot. Bolls never
burst open and fall off prematurely. Sometimes rotting is external (pericarp
rots, internal tissues remain free). Large numbers of fungal fruiting
bodies observed on affected bolls, depending on the fungi involved.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Heavy rainfall during square and boll formation;
wounds caused by insects, especially red cotton bug (Dysdercus cingulata);
close spacing and excessive nitrogen application.
DISEASE CYCLE: Fungi survive in infected bolls in soil. Insects mainly help
spread the disease by creating entry wounds. Secondary spread also via
air-borne conidia.
MANAGEMENT:
- Adopt optimum spacing.
- Apply recommended fertilizer doses.
- Spray Copper oxychloride 2.5 kg/ha with a bollworm insecticide from the
  45th day at 15-day intervals (2-3 sprays).

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Leaf Blight
DISEASE GROUP: Fungal
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Alternaria macrospora
SYMPTOMS: Occurs at all stages, more severe at 45-60 days old. Small,
plate-to-brown, irregular/round spots (0.5-6 mm diameter) with a central
lesion surrounded by concentric rings. Spots coalesce into blighted areas;
affected leaves become brittle and fall off. Stem lesions sometimes seen;
in severe cases spots appear on bracts and bolls.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: High humidity; intermittent rains; moderate
temperature of 25-28°C.
DISEASE CYCLE: Survives in dead leaves as dormant mycelium. Primary spread
through irrigation water; secondary spread mainly by airborne conidia.
MANAGEMENT:
- Remove and destroy infected plant residues.
- Spray Mancozeb 2 kg/ha or Copper oxychloride 2 kg/ha at disease onset.
- Give 4-5 sprays at 15-day intervals.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Bacterial Blight
DISEASE GROUP: Bacterial
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Xanthomonas campestris pv. malvacearum
SYMPTOMS: Attacks all stages from seed to harvest, with five common phases:
1) Seedling blight - small water-soaked, circular/irregular lesions on
   cotyledons; infection spreads via petiole to stem causing withering and
   death of seedlings.
2) Angular leaf spot - small dark green water-soaked areas on lower leaf
   surface, enlarging into angular spots restricted by veins; turn reddish
   brown with age; spreads to veins/veinlets.
3) Vein blight / vein necrosis / black vein - blackening of veins/veinlets
   ("blighting" appearance); bacterial oozes form crusts/scales on lower
   leaf surface; leaves crinkle, twist inward, wither; spreads to petiole
   causing defoliation.
4) Black arm - dark brown to black lesions on stem/fruiting branches,
   girdling stem and branches; premature leaf drop, stem cracking, gummosis,
   stem breakage, hanging as dry black twig ("black arm" symptom).
5) Square rot / Boll rot - water-soaked lesions on bolls turning dark
   black, sunken, irregular; spreads to entire boll causing shedding;
   premature bursting of mature bolls; lint stained yellow by bacterial
   ooze, losing market value; seeds infected, reduced size/viability.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Optimum soil temperature 28°C; high atmospheric
temperature 30-40°C; relative humidity 85%; early sowing; delayed thinning;
poor tillage; late irrigation; potassium deficiency in soil; rain followed
by bright sunshine during October-November.
DISEASE CYCLE: Survives on infected, dried plant debris in soil for
several years. Seed-borne, persisting as slimy mass on seed coat fuzz.
Also attacks Thumbergia thespesioides, Eriodendron anfructuosum, and
Jatropha curcus. Primary infection mainly from seed-borne bacterium.
Secondary spread via wind, wind-blown rain splash, irrigation water,
insects, and implements.
MANAGEMENT:
- Delint cotton seeds with concentrated sulphuric acid at 100 ml/kg; treat
  delinted seeds with carboxin/oxycarboxin at 2 g/kg, or soak in 1000 ppm
  Streptomycin sulphate overnight.
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris.
- Rogue out volunteer cotton plants and weed hosts.
- Follow crop rotation with non-host crops.
- Early thinning and early earthing up with potash.
- Grow resistant varieties.
- Spray Streptomycin sulphate + Tetracycline mixture 100 g with Copper
  oxychloride at 1.25 kg/ha.

CROP: Cotton
DISEASE NAME: Leaf Curl Disease
DISEASE GROUP: Viral
CAUSAL ORGANISM: Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV)
SYMPTOMS: Downward and upward curling of leaves, thickening of veins, and
enation (outgrowth) on the underside of leaves. In severe infection all
leaves are curled and growth is retarded; boll-bearing capacity is reduced.
FAVOURABLE CONDITIONS: Not separately specified; linked to whitefly vector
population buildup.
DISEASE CYCLE: Primary source is the viruliferous whitefly vector Bemisia
tabaci. Alternate and cultivated hosts serve as virus reservoirs throughout
the year.
MANAGEMENT:
- Time planting date to avoid peak vector population.
- Eliminate volunteer perennial cotton and alternate hosts including
  malvaceous weeds like wild okra.
- Foliar application of neem leaf extract and 1% neem oil (achieved ~80%
  reduction in virus transmission in trials).
- Vector management using granular systemic insecticides.
